Every year around tax season, the group chat lights up: *"new regime ya old regime, bro?"* And every year someone confidently gives the wrong answer. Let's fix that with a framework you can actually reason about. 🧾

## The one-line difference

- **New regime:** lower tax rates, but you give up almost all deductions and exemptions. It's the *default* now — if you do nothing, you're on it.
- **Old regime:** higher rates, but you can subtract a pile of deductions first — 80C (PPF, ELSS, EPF, life insurance), HRA, home-loan interest, 80D health insurance, and more.

So it's a trade: **lower rate on your full income** vs **higher rate on a smaller, deduction-shrunk income.** Whichever leaves more in your pocket wins.

## The mental model that decides it

Ask yourself one thing: **do you actually claim a lot of deductions?**

- **You claim a lot** (big 80C + HRA because you pay rent + home-loan interest + health insurance)? → the **old regime** often wins, because all those subtractions can outweigh its higher rates.
- **You claim little to nothing** (no rent/HRA, no home loan, you weren't going to lock money into 80C anyway)? → the **new regime** usually wins on its lower rates alone.

Rough rule of thumb: the more your total deductions add up, the more the old regime pulls ahead. Below a certain deduction threshold, the new regime's lower rates simply win. Where exactly that break-even sits depends on your income and the current slabs.

## Don't guess the exact number — use the official calculator

Here's the honest bit: **slab rates and thresholds get revised in most Union Budgets**, so any specific figure quoted in a blog goes stale fast. Don't take a random number's word for it — punch your actual salary and deductions into the **Income Tax Department's official tax calculator** (on incometax.gov.in). It compares both regimes for *your* numbers in about two minutes. That's the only "which regime" answer that's truly yours.

## Where this touches your investing

One thing worth knowing: tax-saving instruments like **PPF and ELSS give you the 80C deduction only under the old regime.** Under the new regime, that ₹1.5 lakh 80C break mostly doesn't apply.

That doesn't mean "pick old regime to invest." It means: **choose the regime that's genuinely cheaper for you first**, then invest for your goals regardless. Investing to chase a tax break you'd have to overpay to unlock is the tail wagging the dog. If you land on the old regime and want to use 80C well, our [PPF vs ELSS](/blog/ppf-vs-elss/) breakdown is the next read.

Bottom line: it's not "new = good, old = bad." It's whichever keeps more of your money — and that's a two-minute calculation, not a vibe.
