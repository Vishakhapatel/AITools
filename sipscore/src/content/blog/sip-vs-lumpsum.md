"SIP vs lumpsum" gets argued about endlessly, usually with more heat than clarity. The honest answer for most salaried investors is calmer than either side admits: **they solve different problems, and you rarely have to pick just one.**

## The real difference

- A **SIP** invests a fixed amount at regular intervals — it matches how a salary arrives, month after month.
- A **lumpsum** invests a large amount all at once — it matches a windfall: a bonus, maturity of an old policy, an inheritance, sale of an asset.

So the question usually answers itself based on *what money you actually have.* If your money arrives monthly, a SIP isn't a strategy choice — it's just reality.

## Why SIP suits salaried life

Beyond convenience, investing steadily every month means you buy across market ups and downs instead of trying to guess the perfect moment. You automatically buy a little more when prices are low and a little less when they're high. It also removes the hardest part of investing — *deciding to do it* — by making it automatic.

## When lumpsum makes sense

If you're sitting on a large amount that's otherwise idle, keeping it out of the market for years "to be safe" has its own cost: time out of the market. Historically, over long horizons, money invested earlier has had more time to compound. So a windfall you don't need soon is often better put to work than left waiting for a dip that may never come — provided the money genuinely matches your risk appetite and time horizon.

## The middle path most people miss

You don't have to choose. Two common approaches combine both:

- **Invest the lumpsum, then keep a monthly SIP running** on top of it — your existing money compounds while new savings keep flowing in.
- **Stagger a large amount in gradually** if investing it all at once makes you nervous, moving it into equity over several months rather than in one go.

Notice these aren't fund recommendations — they're just *how* you phase money in. The choice of what to invest in is a separate decision.
