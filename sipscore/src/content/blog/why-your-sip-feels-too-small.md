You punch your goal into a SIP calculator, it spits out "₹42,000/month", and your stomach drops. Your whole surplus is ₹35,000. Now what?

Most calculators leave you exactly there — with a number you can't act on. The missing piece isn't the SIP you *need*. It's the honest comparison with the SIP you can *afford*, and what to do when the two don't meet.

## The three numbers that matter

1. **The required SIP** — what the goal demands, after adjusting for inflation.
2. **Your surplus** — income minus expenses.
3. **Your comfortable surplus** — the part of that surplus you can commit without living on the edge. A useful rule of thumb is about 70%, leaving a buffer for emergencies and other goals.

When the required SIP fits inside your comfortable surplus, you're in good shape. When it fits inside your total surplus but not the comfortable part, it's *tight but doable*. When it's more than your whole surplus, the goal isn't affordable **yet** — and that word matters.

## "Not affordable yet" is a starting point, not a verdict

A goal being out of reach today doesn't mean it's out of reach. You have levers:

- **Time.** Extending the horizon even a few years does a lot of the heavy lifting, because compounding has longer to work.
- **The target.** A slightly smaller goal can be dramatically more reachable.
- **Step-ups.** Increasing your SIP a little each year — as your salary grows — closes gaps that a flat SIP never will.
- **Surplus.** A raise, a side income, or trimming a recurring expense all widen the gap you can invest.

## The point

A calculator that only shows the required number is doing half the job. The useful version shows you the gap *and* the ways to close it — so you leave with a plan instead of a panic.
